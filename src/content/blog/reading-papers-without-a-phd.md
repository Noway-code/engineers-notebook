The mistake is reading a paper like a book. Abstract, intro, related work,
and by section 3 you've absorbed nothing and decided you're not smart enough.

## The order that works

Abstract, then figures, then the evaluation, then — only if it still seems
worth it — the actual approach. Figures and graphs carry most of the claim.
The evaluation tells you what the authors think they proved, and what they
quietly declined to measure.

Related work is the most useful section and nobody reads it. It's a curated
map of everything adjacent, written by people who had to read all of it.

Most papers are not worth your full attention. Reading in this order lets you
find that out in ten minutes instead of two hours.
